Upcoming In research & design

Visual Discovery App

A Pinterest-style app built from what people are actually saying about the current one: feeds flooded with low-quality AI-generated posts, ads crowding out real inspiration, and human-made photos, recipes, and art getting buried. I am researching those comments first, then designing a cleaner visual board product around them — not a clone with a new coat of paint.

  • What users keep repeating: r/Pinterest, WIRED, CNN, and 404 Media all point to the same problems — “AI slop” in search and home feeds, affiliate spam after you tap a pin, and ads that can make up a large share of what you see. Pinterest’s own Gen AI labels and “see fewer” tools still miss a lot of synthetic posts, and they often do not apply to ads.
  • Product rules I am designing against that: Human-made images first. AI content labelled in the feed, not only after a tap — or kept off the default board entirely. Far fewer ads, and no bait pins that open an ad-stuffed blog. Search that returns real photos, recipes, and art instead of generated lookalikes.
  • How I will build it: Native Swift / SwiftUI in Xcode — a full iOS app, not a web wrap. Complete frontend and backend so boards, accounts, and uploads are not sitting behind an open client. Auth, security rules, and no API keys in the binary, so one jailbroken phone cannot read everyone else’s pins. UI aimed at 2026 iOS: Liquid Glass, Dynamic Type, Dark Mode, and SF Symbols, tested on device the same way I shipped the FYP on iPhone 14 / iOS 26.

Status: Research and early product design. Prototype and live URL to follow.

Coming soon · App
Upcoming In design & development

Wedding platform

A wedding app I am designing and building as one place to plan — the features people already expect from well-known wedding apps, plus three that those products do not treat as first-class: AI product search in chat, AI invitations generated in the app, and a memory calendar that turns uploads into a slideshow.

  • AI product chat: Search for dresses, venues, flowers, favours, and other wedding products in conversation on the app — ask in plain language, get options back in the thread, instead of leaving to a separate shop or browser tab.
  • AI invitations: Generate invitations in the app from names, date, venue, and style. Edit and send from the same place rather than exporting to a print or stationery site to start over.
  • Memory calendar: Pick any day, upload photos and videos, and keep that day’s memories on a shared calendar. Those uploads become a slideshow — engagement through the wedding and after, not only a dump of the big day.
  • What well-known wedding apps already do: Guest list and RSVPs, seating, budget, vendor shortlists, checklists and timelines, a wedding website, and a registry. Those stay in the product so couples are not stitching five apps together; the three above are what I am adding on top.
  • How I will build it: Native Swift / SwiftUI in Xcode, with a complete frontend and backend — guest lists, RSVPs, photos, and chat are not a local demo. Auth, least-privilege storage, and security rules so couples’ memories and guest data are not readable from the client. No secrets in the app binary; API keys stay on the server. UI to 2026 iOS standards: Liquid Glass, large Dynamic Type, Dark Mode, and a calendar/slideshow that feels native, not a 2019 form stack. Same device-test habit as the birthday social app and FYP.

Status: Design and development. Prototype and live URL to follow.

Where I stand

The AI landscape I’m studying

This is the context behind my focus on agents and models — what I’m seeing, not a product pitch.

From where I sit as someone learning this field every day, US-made AI is not landing well. Public conversation around American AI products and companies is dominated by criticism. There is far more hostility than affection — more hate than love.

The models that are performing well, and that people actually seem to like, are Chinese: DeepSeek, Kimi, and others in that wave. They are strong in use, and they have public goodwill. I do not see the same backlash directed at Kimi or DeepSeek that follows US AI labs and products.

That gap is not only about model quality. Trade pressure, tariffs, and a confrontational stance toward other countries under the Trump administration have had a devastating impact on AI companies in the United States — disrupting supply chains, talent, and the international collaboration those labs depend on. At the same time, open and well-liked models from China are gaining ground with people who actually use the tools.

That is why my main focus is AI: building agents and automation on models that work, that people trust, and that I can learn from in public — not chasing a brand that the public has already turned against.

AI Agents

The work I care about most — systems that plan, use tools, and finish multi-step jobs with less hand-holding.

Main focus · Learning
Active learning Now

Agents that do real work

I want to design agents that can break a goal into steps, call tools and APIs, keep memory, and recover when something fails — not chat demos that forget the last turn.

  • Architecture: Planning, tool use, memory, and evaluation so an agent is useful outside a notebook.
  • Models: Studying how strong public models such as DeepSeek and Kimi behave as the reasoning layer — capability, cost, and how people actually feel about using them.
  • Reliability: Guardrails, logging, and tests, so an agent can be trusted with a real workflow.

This is the track I spend the most time on. Everything else on this page either feeds it or sits beside it.

Primary focus

AI Automation

Connecting models to work that already exists — pipelines, APIs, and jobs that run without someone sitting in a chat box.

Pipelines · Learning
Active learning Now

From prompts to systems

Automation is how AI leaves the playground. I am learning to wire models into real processes: content pipelines, scheduled jobs, APIs, and hand-offs between tools so a result appears without a person prompting every step.

  • Workflows: Triggers, retries, and clear inputs/outputs — so a job can run overnight, not only when I am watching.
  • Content systems: Drafting, sorting, and routing material with a model in the loop, then checking it before it goes anywhere public.
  • Integration: APIs, webhooks, and existing software, so automation sits on top of work I already do rather than replacing it with a new silo.

Agents decide; automation delivers. I am studying both because one without the other stays a demo.

Gaming

Systems · Learning
Active learning Now

Games as systems, not only play

I am interested in gaming as something to build and study, not only consume. Tools have changed: Claude and ChatGPT can already help design systems, write gameplay logic, and prototype — and I want to learn how to use that well inside real engines, without flattening what makes a game good.

  • AI as a co-builder: Claude and ChatGPT can draft mechanics, dialogue, scripts, and small playable loops. I am learning to treat them as production help — iterate, test, and keep the design authored.
  • Engines: Godot, GDevelop, and Unity — from visual tools that get a prototype running quickly, to a full engine I can ship from.
  • Design: Rules, difficulty, feel, and feedback loops that keep a player in the experience.
  • AI in the game: NPCs, tooling, and content aids, used carefully so the game still feels made by someone, not dumped by a model.

This sits beside the AI work. I am learning it because I care about it, not because it is a checkbox on a CV.

Marketing

How products get found, trusted, and talked about — especially in a market where public feeling toward AI brands is already split.

Growth · Learning
Active learning Now

Attention, trust, and honest positioning

Building something is not the same as anyone caring that it exists. I am learning marketing so I can explain work clearly, find the right audience, and avoid the tone-deaf launch culture that has fuelled so much of the backlash against US AI products.

  • Positioning: What a product is for, who it is for, and why someone should trust it — in plain language.
  • Content: Writing and systems that help a project get found without sounding like a press release.
  • AI and marketing: Using models to draft and organise, while keeping a human check on anything that goes out. Public love is earned; public hate is easy.

Watching DeepSeek and Kimi attract genuine goodwill, while US AI draws far more hostility than warmth, is part of why this track matters to me. Sentiment is not a side issue — it is the market.
